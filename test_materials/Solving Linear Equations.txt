# Solving Linear Equations

A linear equation is one where the unknown (usually x) appears only to the power
of one. There are no x-squared or higher terms. The goal is always the same:
get x on its own on one side of the equals sign.

## The golden rule

Whatever you do to one side of the equation, you must do to the other. The
equals sign means both sides are worth exactly the same, and they stay equal
only if you treat them identically.

## One-step equations

If x + 7 = 12, subtract 7 from both sides to get x = 5.
If 4x = 20, divide both sides by 4 to get x = 5.

## Two-step equations

Deal with addition or subtraction first, then multiplication or division.

Example: 2x + 7 = 19
- Subtract 7 from both sides: 2x = 12
- Divide both sides by 2: x = 6

Example: 5x - 3 = 12
- Add 3 to both sides: 5x = 15
- Divide both sides by 5: x = 3

## Watch the signs

A very common mistake is dividing by a negative number and keeping the answer
positive. If -3x = 12, divide both sides by -3 to get x = -4, NOT x = 4.
The negative sign does not disappear.

## Checking your answer

Substitute your value back into the original equation. If both sides are equal,
your answer is correct. For 2x + 7 = 19 with x = 6: 2(6) + 7 = 12 + 7 = 19. Correct.
