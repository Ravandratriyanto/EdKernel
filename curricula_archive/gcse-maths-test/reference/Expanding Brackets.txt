# Expanding Brackets

Expanding brackets means multiplying every term inside the bracket by the term
outside it. This removes the brackets and rewrites the expression as a sum of
separate terms.

## A single bracket

Multiply the term outside by each term inside.

Example: 3(x + 2) = 3 times x + 3 times 2 = 3x + 6.

## Two brackets added together

Expand each bracket separately, then collect like terms.

Example: 3(x + 2) + 2(x - 1)
- Expand the first: 3x + 6
- Expand the second: 2x - 2
- Add them: 3x + 6 + 2x - 2
- Collect like terms: 5x + 4

Be careful with the second bracket: 2 times -1 is -2, not +2. A sign slip here
is one of the most common mistakes in the whole topic.

## Multiplying two brackets together

Every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second. A useful
memory aid is FOIL: First, Outer, Inner, Last.

Example: (x + 3)(x + 2)
- First: x times x = x squared
- Outer: x times 2 = 2x
- Inner: 3 times x = 3x
- Last: 3 times 2 = 6
- Collect: x squared + 2x + 3x + 6 = x squared + 5x + 6

## Collecting like terms

Like terms have the same letter to the same power. 2x and 3x are like terms and
add to 5x. But x squared and x are not like terms and cannot be combined.
